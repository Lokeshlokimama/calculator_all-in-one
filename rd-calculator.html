What this calculator does

A recurring deposit (RD) lets you save a fixed amount every month for a set number of months and earn a fixed rate on it. Enter the monthly instalment, the annual rate and the number of months. The calculator returns the maturity value, the total of your deposits, and the interest earned.

How the interest is worked out

Each instalment earns interest from the month it is paid until maturity, so the first instalment earns the most and the last earns the least. Banks compound RD interest quarterly. This calculator converts the quarterly rate into an equivalent monthly rate and then adds up every instalment’s growth, assuming each deposit is made at the start of its month:

Monthly rate m = (1 + r ÷ 4)1/3 − 1

Maturity = P × (1 + m) × ((1 + m)n − 1) ÷ m

P is the monthly deposit, r the annual rate as a decimal and n the number of months.

Worked example

Deposit 5,000 at the start of each month for 24 months at 6.5% a year:

  • Total deposited = 5,000 × 24 = 1,20,000
  • Maturity value = 1,28,425.30
  • Interest earned = 8,425.30

A smaller check: 2,000 a month for 12 months at 7% gives 24,924.26, of which 924.26 is interest.

Why RD interest looks lower than the rate

In the example, 8,425 of interest on 1,20,000 looks like only 7% over two years. That is not a mistake. Your money was not all invested for the whole period: the last instalment was in the account for just one month. On average, an RD balance is invested for about half the term, so RD interest is roughly half of what a lump-sum FD of the same total would earn.

Understanding each input

  • Monthly deposit — the fixed instalment, above zero.
  • Interest rate — the annual RD rate for your tenure.
  • Time period — in whole months, from 1 to 1200. Banks usually offer 6 months to 10 years.

Assumptions and limitations

The result assumes every instalment is paid on time at the start of the month and the rate stays fixed. Banks charge a penalty for missed instalments and may close the RD after several defaults. Some banks calculate RD maturity with slightly different day-count conventions, so expect small differences from the bank’s schedule. Interest is taxable at your slab rate and subject to TDS, which this calculator does not deduct.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the period in years instead of months.
  • Expecting RD interest to equal rate × total deposits × years. Later instalments earn for less time.
  • Comparing an RD with a SIP using only the headline rate. A SIP has market risk and no guaranteed rate; an RD has a fixed rate.

FAQs

Is RD better than FD?

If you already have the lump sum, an FD at the same rate earns more because all the money is invested from day one. An RD suits saving from monthly income.

Is RD interest compounded quarterly?

Yes, at most Indian banks and the post office, which this calculator assumes.

What happens if I miss an instalment?

Banks usually charge a small penalty per missed month, and repeated defaults can lead to premature closure. The calculator assumes no missed payments.

Is RD interest taxable?

Yes. It is taxed as income at your slab rate, and TDS applies above the threshold.

Why does my bank show a slightly different maturity?

Banks may use day counts or round at each quarter. Differences of a few rupees are normal; your bank’s RD schedule is final.
