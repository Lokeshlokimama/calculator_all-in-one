What this calculator does

Job offers in India are usually quoted as cost to company (CTC), but CTC is not what you are paid each month. Enter the annual CTC, the share of it that is basic salary, the monthly income tax (TDS) from your payslip, and any other monthly deductions. The calculator shows monthly CTC, the employee provident fund (PF) deduction it assumes, the tax you entered, and an estimated in-hand amount.

Formula used

Monthly CTC = Annual CTC ÷ 12

Basic = Monthly CTC × Basic %

Employee PF = Basic × 12%

In-hand = Monthly CTC − Employee PF − Monthly tax − Other deductions

Worked example

An offer of 12,00,000 a year with basic at 40% of CTC, 5,000 monthly TDS on the payslip and 1,000 of other deductions:

  • Monthly CTC = 12,00,000 ÷ 12 = 1,00,000
  • Basic = 1,00,000 × 40% = 40,000
  • Employee PF = 40,000 × 12% = 4,800
  • In-hand = 1,00,000 − 4,800 − 5,000 − 1,000 = 89,200 a month

Why your real in-hand is usually lower

This is a simplified model and, on purpose, it is generous. CTC typically includes money you do not receive monthly:

  • Employer PF contribution (another 12% of basic) goes to your PF account, not your salary.
  • Gratuity provision, about 4.81% of basic, is paid only when you leave after qualifying service.
  • Variable pay or bonus is paid later, and may be paid partly or not at all.
  • Insurance premiums and other benefits are costs to the employer, not cash to you.
  • Professional tax is deducted monthly in many states.

Add these to “other monthly deductions”, or take them out of CTC first, for a more realistic figure. The most accurate source is the salary breakup in your offer letter or a recent payslip.

Understanding each input

  • Annual CTC — the yearly figure from the offer letter.
  • Basic salary % — usually 40–50% of CTC; it drives PF. From 0 to 100.
  • Monthly tax from payslip — this tool does not calculate income tax. Use the TDS on your payslip, or 0 if none.
  • Other monthly deductions — professional tax, insurance, loans, canteen and so on.

Assumptions and limitations

PF is calculated as 12% of the basic share you enter. Many employers cap PF at 12% of a 15,000 wage ceiling (1,800 a month) unless you opt for full PF, so your PF may be lower and in-hand higher. The tool does not pick a tax regime or apply slabs, rebates or deductions. Treat the result as an estimate for comparing offers, not a payslip.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing CTC by 12 and calling it monthly salary.
  • Ignoring variable pay, which can be a large share of CTC at senior levels.
  • Leaving tax at 0 on a salary that clearly attracts TDS.

FAQs

What is CTC?

Cost to company: everything the employer spends on you in a year, including salary, employer PF, gratuity provision, insurance and variable pay.

Why is PF 12% of basic?

Under the EPF scheme both employee and employer contribute 12% of basic wages (basic plus DA). Only the employee share is deducted from your pay.

Does this calculate income tax?

No. Enter the monthly TDS from your payslip. Tax depends on the regime you choose and your deductions.

My PF is capped at 1,800. How do I model that?

Set basic % so that basic is 15,000, or add the difference back. A future version may add a PF cap option.

Is this useful for comparing two offers?

Yes, as long as you use the same assumptions for both, and check how much of each CTC is variable.
