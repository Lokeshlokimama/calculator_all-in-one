What this calculator does

Enter a voltage in volts and a current in amperes. The calculator multiplies them to give power in watts. It is useful for reading appliance labels, sizing a fuse or MCB, checking whether a socket or extension board can take a load, and for DC circuits such as batteries, solar panels and LED strips.

Formula used

Power (W) = Voltage (V) × Current (A)

This is exact for DC circuits, and for AC loads that are purely resistive, such as heaters, geysers, irons, kettles and incandescent bulbs.

Worked example

A water heater draws 5 A from a 230 V household supply.

  • Power = 230 × 5 = 1,150 W, or about 1.15 kW

Run for one hour, it uses 1.15 units (kWh) of electricity.

When power factor matters

Motors, compressors, fans, pumps and many electronic power supplies do not draw current exactly in step with the voltage. For these AC loads the real power is lower than V × I:

Real power (W) = V × I × power factor

A fan motor drawing 5 A at 230 V with a power factor of 0.8 uses 920 W of real power, even though V × I is 1,150. The 1,150 figure is the apparent power in volt-amperes (VA), which is what wiring, inverters and generators must be able to carry. Three-phase supplies need a different formula again (√3 × Vline × I × PF), which this tool does not cover.

Practical uses

  • Socket and extension limits: a 6 A socket at 230 V supports about 1,380 W; a 16 A power socket about 3,680 W.
  • Battery systems: a 12 V system feeding 10 A delivers 120 W.
  • Checking labels: if a charger says 5 V and 3 A, it can deliver up to 15 W.

Limitations and safety

This is an arithmetic aid, not a wiring design tool. Cable sizing, breaker selection and earthing depend on standards, installation method and cable length. For any work on mains wiring, use a licensed electrician.

Common mistakes

  • Using V × I for motors and compressors and overestimating real consumption.
  • Mixing milliamps and amps: 500 mA is 0.5 A.
  • Applying single-phase arithmetic to a three-phase supply.

FAQs

How many watts is 1 amp at 230 volts?

230 W for a resistive load. With a power factor below 1, the real power is lower.

What is the difference between W and VA?

Watts measure real power that does work. Volt-amperes are V × I. They are equal only when the power factor is 1.

Can I use this for DC?

Yes. For DC circuits P = V × I is exact.

How do I find current from watts?

Divide watts by volts. A 2,000 W heater on 230 V draws about 8.7 A. The Ohm’s law calculator does this for you.

Does this work for three-phase?

No. Three-phase power needs the √3 factor and line voltage.
