What this calculator does

Enter your weight and choose an activity level. The calculator multiplies weight by 35 ml per kilogram and an activity multiplier to give an illustrative daily fluid amount in litres.

Formula used

Fluid (litres) = Weight (kg) × 0.035 × Activity multiplier

Multipliers: light activity 1.0, moderate 1.2, high 1.5.

Worked example

A 70 kg adult with moderate activity: 70 × 0.035 × 1.2 = 2.94, shown as 2.9 litres a day. At light activity the same person gets 2.45, shown as 2.5 litres.

Read this before using the number

There is no single correct amount of water. Rules of thumb like this one are simple averages. Health authorities give broad ranges instead: the European Food Safety Authority, for example, considers about 2.0 litres a day for women and 2.5 litres for men adequate as total water, which includes the water in food (often 20–30% of intake). The NHS suggests 6 to 8 glasses of fluid a day for most people in the UK climate.

Your needs rise in hot and humid weather, which is much of India for much of the year, with physical work or exercise, and with fever, vomiting or diarrhoea. Pale yellow urine is a simple everyday sign of being well hydrated.

When not to use this

Some people are told by their doctor to limit fluids, for example with heart failure, some kidney conditions or liver disease. Follow that advice, not this calculator. Pregnant and breastfeeding women, older adults and children also have different needs. Drinking very large amounts quickly can be harmful.

What counts as fluid

Water, milk, buttermilk, tea, coffee, coconut water and soups all count. Fruit and vegetables add more. Sugary drinks add fluid but also a lot of sugar.

FAQs

How much water should I drink a day?

It varies. This tool gives a rough weight-based figure; general guidance for adults is around 2–2.5 litres of total water a day, more in heat or with activity.

Do tea and coffee count?

Yes. Moderate amounts of tea and coffee count towards fluid intake.

Does food count towards water intake?

Yes. Food supplies a meaningful share of total water, which this simple rule does not separate.

I have a kidney or heart condition. Should I use this?

No. Follow your doctor’s advice on fluids.

Can you drink too much water?

Yes. Very large amounts in a short time can lower blood sodium dangerously. Spread intake through the day.
