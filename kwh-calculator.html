What this calculator does

Enter the appliance’s power rating in watts and how many hours it runs. The calculator returns the energy used in kilowatt-hours (kWh). One kWh is one “unit” on an Indian electricity bill.

Formula used

Energy (kWh) = Power (W) × Hours ÷ 1,000

Dividing by 1,000 converts watt-hours into kilowatt-hours.

Worked example

A 1,500 W air conditioner running for 8 hours at its full rating:

  • Energy = 1,500 × 8 ÷ 1,000 = 12 kWh (12 units)

At 7 per unit, that night costs about 84. Over a 30-night month it would be 360 units, which is why AC use often pushes households into a higher tariff slab.

Rated watts vs real use

The label wattage is the maximum, not the average. Appliances with thermostats or compressors switch on and off:

  • An inverter AC at a moderate setting may average 40–70% of its rated power once the room is cool.
  • A refrigerator’s compressor runs only part of each hour; its yearly energy figure on the BEE star label is a better guide than watts × 24.
  • Fans, lights, TVs and chargers run close to their rating while on.

For a more realistic number, reduce the hours to the time the appliance actually draws full power, or use the BEE label’s kWh per year.

Understanding each input

  • Appliance power — in watts, from the label or manual. For kW ratings multiply by 1,000; 1.5 kW is 1,500 W.
  • Hours used — total running time. Use decimals for minutes: 30 minutes is 0.5.

Limitations

The result is energy, not cost. Indian tariffs are usually slab-based, so the cost of a unit depends on your total monthly consumption, plus fixed charges and duties. Use the electricity bill calculator for a full bill estimate.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to divide by 1,000 and reading watt-hours as units.
  • Using the rated power for appliances that cycle on and off.
  • Multiplying by a flat rate when the tariff is slab-based.

FAQs

What is 1 unit of electricity?

One kilowatt-hour: 1,000 watts used for one hour, such as a 100 W bulb for 10 hours.

How many units does a 1.5 ton AC use?

It depends on its star rating, inverter technology and settings. At a full 1,500 W it uses 1.5 units an hour; real averages are usually lower.

How do I convert kWh to cost?

Multiply by your per-unit rate, remembering slab tariffs. The electricity bill calculator handles slabs.

Is kWh the same as kW?

No. kW is power (how fast energy is used); kWh is energy (power over time).

How do I estimate monthly use?

Use the watt to unit calculator, which multiplies by hours per day and days in the month.
