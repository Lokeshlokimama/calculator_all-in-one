Safety note

Browser merge

This tool copies pages but does not repair damaged files, bypass passwords, preserve every advanced annotation, or verify legal completeness. Open the merged PDF before sending it.

How to merge PDFs without losing control of the document

A merge tool is useful when related pages are scattered across separate files, but the final PDF should still be checked like a finished document.

1

Prepare the order first

Put cover letters, forms, supporting documents, and appendices in the same order you want the reader to see them. Rename files with numbers if your browser sorts them alphabetically.

2

Use compatible PDFs

Normal PDFs merge best. Password-protected, corrupted, signed, or unusually complex PDFs may fail or lose advanced features such as forms, embedded media, or special annotations.

3

Open the output

Before sending the merged file, check the first and last page, total page count, page order, and whether the important attachments are present.

Common merge use cases

Document bundles

Merge PDFs when you need one upload for invoices, bank statements, school submissions, job applications, client paperwork, travel documents, or project notes. For legal, medical, tax, or signed documents, keep the original files separately and verify whether the receiving organization accepts a browser-created merged copy.

FAQs

Can it merge password-protected PDFs?

No. Unlock protected PDFs with software you trust before merging.

Does merge change the original files?

No. Original PDFs stay untouched; the tool creates a new merged download.

Can I reorder after selecting?

This version uses picker order. Select files in the desired order or rename them before upload.
