What this calculator does

Enter your sex, height and body measurements in centimetres. Women also enter hip circumference. The calculator estimates the percentage of your body weight that is fat.

Formula used

Developed by Hodgdon and Beckett for the US Navy, using measurements in centimetres:

Men: Body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)) − 450

Women: Body fat % = 495 ÷ (1.29579 − 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height)) − 450

Worked examples

  • Man, 175 cm tall, neck 38 cm, waist 88 cm: about 19.2%.
  • Woman, 162 cm tall, neck 32 cm, waist 76 cm, hip 98 cm: about 30.3%.

How to measure

  • Neck: just below the larynx (Adam’s apple), tape sloping slightly down at the front.
  • Waist: men at the navel; women at the narrowest point of the waist. Relax and breathe out normally; do not pull in.
  • Hip (women): around the widest part of the buttocks.
  • Keep the tape level and snug but not compressing the skin. Measure twice and use the average.

How accurate is it?

Tape-based methods are estimates. Compared with lab methods such as DEXA scans, the Navy method is often within a few percentage points for many people, but errors can be larger for very lean, very muscular or higher-weight people. A 1 cm change in waist measurement moves the result noticeably, so measuring consistently matters more than the exact number. It is most useful for tracking change over time.

Putting the number in context

Healthy body fat ranges depend on age and sex; women naturally carry more essential fat than men. Rather than chasing a specific percentage, many clinicians watch waist circumference, because fat around the abdomen is linked with diabetes and heart disease risk. Indian guidelines flag a waist above about 90 cm for men and 80 cm for women as higher risk.

FAQs

Is the Navy body fat method accurate?

It is a reasonable estimate for many people, often within a few percentage points, but less reliable for very muscular or very lean people.

Why do women need a hip measurement?

The women’s equation was developed with hip circumference because fat distribution differs between sexes.

Can I use inches?

Convert to centimetres first: inches × 2.54.

Why does the result say Error?

The measurements are outside what the formula can use, often because the waist is not larger than the neck. Re-measure.

How often should I measure?

Every few weeks under the same conditions is enough to see trends.
