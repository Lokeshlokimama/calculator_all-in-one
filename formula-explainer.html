What this page does

Choose one of three formulas from the menu to see each symbol labelled. The sections below go further, showing what each formula means and putting real numbers into it. They are chosen because each one turns up constantly: in physics class, in personal finance, and in explaining why things fall.

E = mc&sup2;: mass and energy

E is energy in joules, m is mass in kilograms, and c is the speed of light, about 3 × 108 metres per second. Because c² is so large (9 × 1016), a tiny mass corresponds to a huge amount of energy.

Example: 1 gram (0.001 kg) of mass is equivalent to 0.001 × 9 × 1016 = 9 × 1013 joules, about 21 kilotonnes of TNT, more than the Hiroshima bomb released. In practice, nuclear power plants and the Sun convert only a small fraction of their fuel’s mass into energy.

A = P(1 + r/n)<sup>nt</sup>: compound interest

A is the final amount, P the principal (starting amount), r the annual interest rate as a decimal, n how many times a year interest is added, and t the number of years. Interest earns interest, so growth speeds up over time.

Example: 1,00,000 at 8% compounded quarterly for 5 years: 1,00,000 × (1 + 0.08 ÷ 4)20 ≈ 1,48,595. The same money at 8% simple interest would reach only 1,40,000. See the FD calculator to try your own numbers.

F = G m<sub>1</sub>m<sub>2</sub> / r&sup2;: gravity

F is the force of attraction in newtons, G is the gravitational constant (6.674 × 10−11 N m²/kg²), m1 and m2 are the two masses in kilograms, and r is the distance between their centres in metres. Doubling the distance cuts the force to a quarter: this is the inverse-square law.

Example: Earth (5.972 × 1024 kg) pulling on a 70 kg person at Earth’s radius (6.371 × 106 m) gives F ≈ 687 N, which is that person’s weight. Dividing by 70 gives about 9.8 m/s², the familiar acceleration due to gravity.

Tips for reading any formula

  • Find what is being calculated: the single symbol on the left.
  • Check the units of every symbol before substituting numbers.
  • Look for powers and denominators: they show what has the biggest effect, such as c² or 1/r².
  • Try an easy number first to see if the answer makes sense.

FAQs

Why is the speed of light squared in E = mc&sup2;?

It comes from the units and the physics of relativity: energy has units of mass × velocity², and c is the conversion factor between mass and energy.

What does n mean in the compound interest formula?

The number of times interest is added each year: 1 for yearly, 4 for quarterly, 12 for monthly.

Why does gravity fall with distance squared?

The force spreads out over the surface of an ever-larger sphere, whose area grows with the square of the radius.
