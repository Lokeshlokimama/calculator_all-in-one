What this calculator does

Enter the deposit amount, the annual interest rate the bank quotes, and the period in years. Choose whether the senior citizen rate applies. The calculator returns the maturity value, the amount deposited and the interest earned.

It models a cumulative fixed deposit, where interest is added back to the deposit every quarter and paid out at the end. That is the reinvestment option most banks offer, and it is what produces the “maturity value” on a bank’s FD receipt.

Formula used

Maturity = P × (1 + r ÷ 4)4 × t

P is the deposit, r is the annual rate as a decimal (plus 0.5 percentage points if senior citizen is selected), and t is the time in years. The 4 represents quarterly compounding.

Worked example

A deposit of 1,00,000 for 5 years at 7% a year, normal rate:

  • Maturity = 1,00,000 × (1 + 0.07 ÷ 4)20 = 1,41,477.82
  • Interest = 41,477.82

With the senior citizen option the rate becomes 7.5% and the maturity rises to 1,44,994.80, about 3,500 more over five years.

Quoted rate vs effective yield

Because interest is compounded four times a year, a 7% FD actually grows by about 7.19% in a year: 1,00,000 becomes 1,07,185.90. Banks sometimes print this “annualised yield” next to the rate. When you compare an FD with a bond, a savings scheme or a mutual fund, compare effective yields, not headline rates.

Understanding each input

  • Deposit amount — the lump sum you place in the FD.
  • Interest rate — the annual rate on the bank’s rate card for your tenure, from 0 to 100.
  • Time period — in years. Use decimals for part years: 18 months is 1.5.
  • Citizen type — the senior option adds 0.5 percentage points, which is the most common extra rate. Some banks offer more or less; if yours differs, choose Normal and type the bank’s actual senior rate.

Assumptions and limitations

The calculator assumes the rate stays fixed, compounding is quarterly, and you do not withdraw early. It does not deduct tax. In India, FD interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate, and banks deduct TDS when interest crosses the annual threshold, so the money you actually receive can be lower. Premature withdrawal usually carries a penalty of around 0.5–1% on the rate. Small finance banks and non-bank deposits can carry different risk from scheduled banks; deposit insurance under DICGC covers up to 5 lakh per depositor per bank.

The tool also labels itself for savings (SB) accounts. Savings accounts in India are usually credited quarterly too, but their rates can change at any time and balances move, so treat an SB result as a rough guide only.

Common mistakes

  • Entering months in the years field. Five years is 5, not 60.
  • Comparing a cumulative FD maturity with a monthly-payout FD. Payout FDs do not compound, so they show lower total interest.
  • Choosing senior citizen and also typing the senior rate, which adds the bonus twice.
  • Forgetting tax when comparing an FD with tax-advantaged options.

FAQs

Is FD interest compounded monthly or quarterly?

Most Indian banks compound cumulative FDs quarterly, which is what this calculator uses. Check your bank’s terms if unsure.

Does the result include TDS?

No. It shows interest before tax. TDS and income tax depend on your total income and the forms you submit to the bank.

How much extra do senior citizens get?

Commonly 0.5 percentage points, which the senior option adds. Some banks offer up to about 0.75 or more on special schemes.

Can I use it for a monthly interest FD?

Not directly. A payout FD pays simple interest each period: deposit × rate ÷ 12 per month. Use the cumulative result only for reinvestment FDs.

Why is my bank&rsquo;s figure slightly different?

Banks may count exact days for short tenures or broken periods and round each quarter. Small differences are normal; the bank’s FD advice is final.
