What this prepayment calculator estimates

The calculator estimates outstanding balance after a number of EMIs, subtracts the lump-sum prepayment, and estimates how many months remain if the EMI continues unchanged.

Why prepayment can help

In reducing-balance loans, interest is charged on outstanding principal. Paying principal early can reduce future interest, especially in the early years of a long loan.

Check lender rules

Some lenders have lock-in periods, part-payment minimums, foreclosure fees, or different rules for fixed and floating rates. Confirm the policy before making a large payment.

Limitations

This is an estimate. It assumes the rate and EMI remain constant and does not model exact payment dates, reset dates, penalties, tax effects, or fees.

Method and limitations

The calculation uses transparent arithmetic from the inputs shown on the page. It does not include lender-specific fees, tax classification, market volatility, eligibility rules, or provider quotations unless you enter those values yourself. FAQs

Is prepayment always best?

Not always. Compare interest saved with liquidity needs, emergency funds, investment alternatives, and lender charges.

Does this reduce EMI or tenure?

This page estimates tenure reduction when EMI stays the same.

Can I use it for home loans?

Yes, as a planning estimate if the loan uses reducing-balance EMI.

Does it include prepayment penalties?

No. Add lender charges separately.

Why are early prepayments powerful?

More outstanding principal remains early in the loan, so principal reduction can affect more future interest.

Is the future payment saved exact?

No. It is a simplified estimate based on fixed-rate math.

Compare a prepayment at the right point in the schedule

Begin with the outstanding balance

The principal field is the original loan amount, while paid months tells the model how many scheduled instalments have already been made. The calculator first follows the repayment sequence to estimate the remaining balance. Only then does it apply the extra payment. This distinction matters: subtracting a prepayment directly from the original principal after several years ignores the principal already repaid through the regular instalments.

For a transparent zero-interest example, start with 120,000 over one year. The regular payment is 10,000. After three paid months, the remaining balance is 90,000. A prepayment of 30,000 leaves 60,000. If the monthly payment stays at 10,000, six payments remain instead of nine, so the term is shortened by three months. There is no interest saving because the assumed rate is zero. This test isolates the balance and timing logic before introducing positive interest.

The same 30,000 extra payment made before the first instalment leaves 90,000 and nine scheduled payments under this model. The total number of payments remaining is different because the comparison is made at a different point in time. Preserve paid months in your notes; an extra-payment amount without a date in the schedule is not enough to reproduce the estimate.

Understand the chosen comparison

This page keeps the existing payment and estimates how much sooner the balance can close. A lender may offer a different arrangement that reduces the payment while retaining the remaining term. Those are different outcomes. Ask which option will be applied before interpreting the model's months-saved result as the lender's plan. The calculator does not negotiate the option or instruct the lender to make the payment.

At a positive rate, each future month's interest depends on the balance entering that month. A prepayment reduces that balance, so it can change the future interest path. The model compares future schedules from the prepayment point; interest already paid is not recovered by making an extra payment today. A displayed interest saving therefore should not be subtracted from historical payments or described as money already returned to the borrower.

The last payment may be smaller than a full monthly instalment when the remaining balance and its interest fit within that payment. A term shortened by an integer number of months is not necessarily a saving of exactly that number times the full EMI. Compare the future-payment totals and any partial final payment rather than reconstructing the saving from the month count alone.

Check the lender's treatment before paying

Request an up-to-date outstanding balance and the date on which the extra payment will be credited. This model assumes scheduled payments and a constant rate, not late instalments, daily accrual, or a rate-reset history. If the account has changed from its original terms, a statement-based calculation can differ materially from one reconstructed using only the original rate.

Fees and contractual conditions also belong in a separate comparison. The tool does not determine whether a prepayment charge applies to your particular loan, whether a minimum extra payment is required, or whether other instructions are necessary. Verify those details with the lender. A mathematically lower interest total is not an assessment of your wider cash needs or the suitability of using savings for a prepayment.

Handle a full payoff and invalid inputs

A proposed prepayment larger than the modelled balance cannot make the loan balance negative. The estimate caps the applied amount at the outstanding balance and treats the remaining duration as zero once that balance is cleared. Any additional amount you entered does not represent an extra interest saving. Obtain an actual payoff quote, including its validity date, for a real closure transaction.

The paid-month count must describe payments already completed, not months still remaining. For a twelve-month model, entering twenty-four paid months is inconsistent and should be rejected. Blank and negative values also need correction before a result is meaningful. If an error appears, do not rely on a previous number still visible elsewhere on the page.

Keep a concise decision record

Save the original principal, nominal annual rate, original months, paid months, proposed extra payment, modelled balance, and remaining-term comparison together. Mark whether the model assumes the payment stays constant. Add the lender's fees and timing as separate facts. This record makes the calculation auditable without implying that the generic estimate is a binding settlement quote.
