What this calculator does

Enter the original (list) price, the discount percentage and the tax percentage. The calculator applies the discount first, then adds tax to the discounted price, and shows the amount saved, the tax added and the final price.

Formula used

Discounted price = Price × (1 − Discount % ÷ 100)

Tax added = Discounted price × Tax % ÷ 100

Final price = Discounted price + Tax added

Worked example

A pair of shoes listed at 2,000 with 20% off, and 18% GST added at billing:

  • Discounted price = 2,000 × 0.80 = 1,600, so you save 400
  • Tax added = 1,600 × 18% = 288
  • Final price = 1,600 + 288 = 1,888

A common shortcut is to subtract 20% and add 18% to the list price together (2,000 × 0.98 = 1,960). That is wrong, because the tax is charged on the discounted amount, not the original.

Order matters, and so does MRP

In India, tax is charged on the transaction value, which is the price after any discount shown on the invoice. That is the order this calculator uses.

Most retail products in India carry an MRP that already includes GST. If a shop gives 20% off an MRP of 2,000, you simply pay 1,600 and no tax is added on top; enter the tax as 0. Use a tax rate only when prices are shown before tax, as on many B2B quotes, hotel tariffs and some online listings.

Understanding each input

  • Original price — the list price before discount.
  • Discount — a percentage from 0 to 100. Leave blank for no discount.
  • Tax — a percentage from 0 to 100, for example 5, 12, 18 or 28 for GST slabs, or a local sales tax rate. Leave blank for none.

Stacked discounts

“20% off plus an extra 10%” is not 30% off. The second discount applies to the already reduced price: 2,000 × 0.8 × 0.9 = 1,440, which is 28% off. To model this, run the first discount, then enter the result as the new price with the second discount.

Common mistakes

  • Adding tax to an MRP that already includes it.
  • Adding discount and tax percentages together.
  • Treating stacked discounts as one combined percentage.

FAQs

Is GST charged before or after discount?

After. GST is charged on the transaction value, which is the price after a discount shown on the invoice.

Do I need to add GST to the MRP?

No. MRP in India already includes all taxes. Enter 0 tax when the discount is on MRP.

How do I find the original price from a discounted price?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). For 1,600 after 20% off: 1,600 ÷ 0.8 = 2,000.

Can I use it for sales tax outside India?

Yes. Enter your local sales tax rate. The order of calculation is the same in most places.

What if I only want to remove GST from a price?

Use the GST calculator’s remove GST mode instead.
