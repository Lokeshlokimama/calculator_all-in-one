What this calculator does

Enter the battery’s capacity in ampere-hours (Ah), its voltage, the total load you want to run in watts, and the inverter’s efficiency. The calculator estimates the backup time in hours and minutes.

Formula used

Backup (hours) = Battery Ah × Battery voltage × Efficiency ÷ Load (W)

Ah × V gives the battery’s stored energy in watt-hours. Efficiency accounts for energy lost in the inverter as it converts battery DC to household AC.

Worked example

A 150 Ah, 12 V tubular battery running 300 W of load (three fans, four LED lights and a Wi-Fi router) through an inverter that is 85% efficient:

  • Backup = 150 × 12 × 0.85 ÷ 300 = 5.1 hours on paper

Why real backup is usually shorter

The formula assumes you can use the battery’s full rated capacity. You usually cannot, and should not:

  • Depth of discharge: lead-acid batteries last much longer if they are not drained below about 50%. Using half the capacity, the example above gives about 2.5 hours of healthy backup.
  • Discharge rate: a battery’s Ah rating is measured over a long discharge (often 20 hours). Heavy loads drain it faster than the simple formula predicts.
  • Age and temperature: capacity falls as batteries age and in very hot rooms.

Lithium batteries tolerate deeper discharge, often 80–90%, so they come closer to the calculated figure.

Understanding each input

  • Battery capacity — the Ah rating on the battery, such as 100, 150 or 200.
  • Battery voltage — 12 V for a single battery. Two 12 V batteries in series make a 24 V system with the same Ah.
  • Load — the sum of the wattages you will run together. Ceiling fan about 75 W (BLDC about 30 W), LED tube about 20 W, TV 60–120 W, Wi-Fi router about 10 W.
  • Efficiency — 80–90% for most home inverters.

What to keep off the inverter

Most home inverters of 700–1,100 VA cannot run geysers, irons, induction cooktops or ACs. Even when a high-wattage appliance runs, it empties the battery very quickly. Check the inverter’s VA rating as well as the battery.

Common mistakes

  • Expecting the full calculated backup from a lead-acid battery.
  • Adding batteries in series and also doubling the Ah.
  • Using the inverter’s VA rating as the load.

FAQs

How long will a 150Ah battery last?

On paper, about 5 hours at 300 W with 85% efficiency. For long battery life with lead-acid, plan for around half of that.

Which battery is better for long power cuts?

Tall tubular lead-acid batteries handle deep cycling better than flat-plate ones. Lithium batteries handle it best but cost more.

How do I calculate my load?

Add the wattage of everything that runs at the same time during a power cut.

What is the difference between this and the UPS calculator?

The maths is the same. The UPS page shows minutes and focuses on computer loads.

Does inverter VA rating affect backup?

Not directly. VA limits how much load you can connect; battery Ah decides how long it lasts.
