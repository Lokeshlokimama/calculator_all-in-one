What this calculator does

Enter the length of one side as the base, and the perpendicular height from that base to the opposite corner. The calculator returns the area in square units.

Formula used

Area = ½ × base × height

This works for every triangle: right-angled, isosceles, equilateral or scalene. A triangle is exactly half of a parallelogram with the same base and height.

Worked example

A triangle with a base of 10 m and a height of 8 m has an area of ½ × 10 × 8 = 40 m².

For a right-angled triangle, the two sides that meet at the right angle are a base and height, so a triangle with legs of 6 cm and 4 cm has an area of 12 cm².

The height must be perpendicular

The height is the straight-line distance at a right angle from the base to the opposite vertex, not the length of a sloping side. In an obtuse triangle the height can fall outside the triangle, on an extension of the base line. If you measure a sloping side instead, the area will be too large.

When you only know the three sides

Use Heron’s formula. Let s = (a + b + c) ÷ 2. Then area = √(s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)). For sides 13, 14 and 15: s = 21 and area = √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = 84. This is useful for land plots measured along their boundaries.

Common mistakes

  • Using a slanted side as the height.
  • Forgetting the ½.
  • Mixing units between base and height.

FAQs

What is the formula for the area of a triangle?

Area = ½ × base × perpendicular height.

Can any side be the base?

Yes, as long as you use the height perpendicular to that side.

How do I find the area of an equilateral triangle?

Area = (√3 ÷ 4) × side², or use base = side and height = side × 0.866.

What if I only know the sides?

Use Heron’s formula, explained above.

What units is the answer in?

The square of whatever unit you used.
