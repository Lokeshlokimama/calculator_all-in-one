What this calculator does

Generators are rated in kVA (apparent power), while appliances are usually rated in watts (real power). Enter the total wattage you need to run at once, the load’s power factor, and a safety margin. The calculator returns the generator size in kVA.

Formula used

kVA = Load (W) ÷ (Power factor × 1,000) × (1 + Margin ÷ 100)

Most generators in India are rated at a power factor of 0.8, which is why 0.8 is the default.

Worked example

A small shop needs 5,000 W of running load. With a power factor of 0.8 and a 25% safety margin:

  • kVA = 5,000 ÷ (0.8 × 1,000) × 1.25 = 7.81 kVA

You would choose the next standard size up, such as a 10 kVA set, especially if any motors are on the list.

Starting load: the part people miss

Motors draw several times their running current for a moment when they start. AC compressors, water pumps, refrigerators, lifts and air compressors can need 3–7 times their running watts at start-up. If the generator cannot supply this surge, the motor stalls or the generator trips.

A practical approach: add up the running watts of everything, then add the extra starting watts of the single largest motor (since motors rarely start at the same instant). Enter that total as the load, or increase the safety margin.

Understanding each input

  • Total load — the sum of the watts that will run together.
  • Power factor — 0.8 is the standard rating assumption; it must be between 0 and 1.
  • Safety margin — 20–25% is common so the generator runs at about 75–80% of capacity, which is better for fuel use and engine life.

Limitations

This gives a first estimate. Three-phase balancing, altitude and temperature derating, cable runs and changeover switch sizing all affect the final choice. For a building or commercial installation, have an electrician or the generator supplier do a load study.

Common mistakes

  • Treating kVA and kW as the same: a 10 kVA generator at 0.8 PF supplies 8 kW.
  • Ignoring motor starting surges.
  • Sizing for the whole connected load when only part of it runs during an outage.

FAQs

What is the difference between kVA and kW?

kW is real power; kVA is apparent power. kW = kVA × power factor.

Why is power factor 0.8?

It is the standard rating point for most generator sets.

How much safety margin should I use?

20–25% is common. Use more if you expect to add load later or have large motors.

Can a 5 kVA generator run a 1.5 ton AC?

Usually yes for running load, but check its starting current; inverter ACs start more gently than fixed-speed ones.

Does this work for three-phase generators?

It gives the total kVA, but the load must also be balanced across phases.
