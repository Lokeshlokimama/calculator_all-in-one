What this calculator does

Enter the original width and height, then either a new width or a new height. The calculator fills in the other dimension so the proportions stay the same, reduces the ratio to its simplest form, and shows the scale as a percentage.

Formula used

New height = New width × Original height ÷ Original width

New width = New height × Original width ÷ Original height

The ratio is simplified by dividing both sides by their greatest common divisor.

Worked examples

  • 1920 × 1080 scaled to a width of 1280 gives a height of 720. The ratio is 16:9 and the scale is 66.67%.
  • A 1080 × 1350 portrait image is 4:5, Instagram’s tallest feed format.

Common aspect ratios

  • 16:9 — HD and 4K video, YouTube, most monitors and TVs
  • 9:16 — vertical video: Reels, Shorts, Stories
  • 4:3 — older displays, many phone photos
  • 1:1 — square social posts
  • 4:5 — portrait social posts
  • 3:2 — many camera sensors and prints

Tips

  • Scaling an image up beyond its original pixel size makes it blurry, even with the right ratio.
  • To change the ratio itself, such as 16:9 to 1:1, you need to crop or add borders; resizing alone will stretch it.
  • For thumbnails, YouTube recommends 1280 × 720.

FAQs

How do I calculate aspect ratio?

Divide width and height by their greatest common divisor. 1920 and 1080 share 120, giving 16:9.

What is 1080p in 16:9?

1920 × 1080.

What size is a 9:16 video?

Commonly 1080 × 1920 pixels.

Can I enter both new width and height?

If you enter a new width, it is used and the height is recalculated.

Does it work in centimetres or inches?

Yes. Ratios work in any unit, as long as both dimensions use the same one.
