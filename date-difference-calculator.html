What this calculator does

Pick a start date and an end date. The calculator returns the number of calendar days between them. The order does not matter; the result is always positive. It counts calendar days in your local time zone, so daylight-saving changes do not create an off-by-one error.

How days are counted

The result is the number of days you would step forward from the start date to reach the end date. From 1 May to 15 May is 14 days. If you need to count both the first and last day, as in “from 1 May to 15 May inclusive”, add 1, giving 15.

Worked examples

  • 1 May 2026 to 15 May 2026: 14 days (15 inclusive)
  • 1 January 2026 to 1 January 2027: 365 days
  • 1 January 2028 to 1 January 2029: 366 days, because 2028 is a leap year

To turn days into weeks, divide by 7: 100 days is 14 weeks and 2 days.

Notice periods and working days

This tool counts every calendar day, including weekends and holidays. Employment notice periods are usually stated in calendar days or months, so calendar counting is often right, but check your contract. For working days, roughly multiply by 5/7 and then subtract public holidays, or count on a calendar. Legal and tax deadlines have their own rules on whether the first day counts and what happens when the last day is a holiday.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to add 1 for inclusive ranges, such as hotel nights versus days of a trip.
  • Assuming every year has 365 days.
  • Counting months as 30 days when the exact date matters.

FAQs

Does it include the end date?

No. Add 1 if you need both the start and end dates counted.

Does it count weekends?

Yes. It counts all calendar days.

Does it handle leap years?

Yes.

Can I find a date a number of days in the future?

Not with this tool; it counts the days between two dates you choose.

How do I find my age in days?

Use your date of birth and today, or the age calculator.
