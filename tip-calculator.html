What this calculator does

Enter the bill amount, the tip percentage you want to leave, and how many people are sharing. The calculator shows the tip in money, the bill total including the tip, and what each person pays if the total is split evenly.

It is meant for the everyday case: a group meal, a shared taxi, a delivery order split between flatmates. It treats everyone as paying the same share.

Formula used

Tip = Bill × Tip % ÷ 100

Total = Bill + Tip

Each person pays = Total ÷ Number of people

Worked example

Four friends have dinner and the bill is 2,400. They agree on a 10% tip.

  • Tip = 2,400 × 10 ÷ 100 = 240
  • Total = 2,400 + 240 = 2,640
  • Each person pays = 2,640 ÷ 4 = 660

Tip on the bill before or after tax?

Enter whichever amount you want the tip to be calculated on. Many people tip on the pre-tax food total, because GST or sales tax is not part of the service. If you tip on the final printed amount, the tip will be slightly larger. Neither is wrong; just be consistent within a group.

Check for a service charge first. In India, a restaurant cannot add a service charge to the bill by default; the Central Consumer Protection Authority’s guidelines make it voluntary, and you can ask for it to be removed. If a service charge is already on the bill and you are happy to pay it, set the tip to 0 or reduce it so you do not tip twice.

Understanding each input

  • Bill amount — the amount to tip on (see above). Zero is allowed.
  • Tip percent — for example 5, 10 or 15. Enter 0 to simply split a bill.
  • People — a whole number of at least 1.

Limitations

The split is even. If people ordered very different amounts, split the items first and use this only for the shared tip. Per-person amounts are rounded to two decimals for display, so the shares can add up to a few paise or cents more or less than the total; one person can round up to cover the difference.

Common mistakes

  • Tipping on top of a service charge that is already included.
  • Entering the tip as a decimal (0.1) instead of a percentage (10).
  • Counting the person paying the card twice, or forgetting them, in the number of people.

FAQs

How much should I tip in India?

There is no fixed rule. Around 5–10% at sit-down restaurants is common where no service charge is billed, and many people round up for delivery or cab drivers. It is always your choice.

Can I use it just to split a bill?

Yes. Set the tip percent to 0 and it divides the bill evenly.

Does it handle uneven splits?

No. It splits evenly. For uneven orders, total each person’s items separately first.

Why do the shares not add up exactly?

Shares are rounded to two decimal places for display. The unrounded shares do add up to the total.

Is a service charge the same as a tip?

A service charge is added by the restaurant; a tip is given by you. In India the service charge is voluntary, so you can ask for it to be removed and tip directly instead.
