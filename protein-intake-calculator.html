What this calculator does

Enter your body weight and choose an activity level or goal. The calculator multiplies your weight by a grams-per-kilogram figure to give an illustrative daily protein amount.

Formula used

Protein (g/day) = Weight (kg) × Ratio (g/kg)

Ratios offered: sedentary 0.8, active 1.2, building muscle 1.6, heavy lifting 2.0.

Worked example

A 70 kg adult:

  • Sedentary: 70 × 0.8 = 56 g
  • Active: 70 × 1.2 = 84 g
  • Building muscle: 70 × 1.6 = 112 g

Where these numbers come from

The 0.8 g/kg figure is close to the general recommended dietary allowance for healthy adults; ICMR–NIN’s 2020 recommendations for Indians use about 0.83 g/kg. Higher figures of 1.2–2.0 g/kg are commonly used in sports nutrition for people doing regular endurance or strength training. Above about 1.6 g/kg, studies show little extra benefit for muscle gain for most people.

Meeting it on an Indian diet

Surveys suggest many Indian diets are high in cereals and fall short on protein. Common sources and approximate protein content:

  • Cooked dal, 1 katori (about 150 g): 7–9 g
  • Paneer, 100 g: about 18 g
  • Curd, 200 g: about 6–8 g
  • Eggs, 1 large: about 6 g
  • Chicken, 100 g cooked: about 25–30 g
  • Soya chunks, 30 g dry: about 15 g
  • Roasted chana, 30 g: about 6 g

Spreading protein across meals is generally recommended.

Who should check with a doctor

People with kidney disease are often advised to limit protein, and pregnant or breastfeeding women, older adults and people recovering from illness may need different amounts. Follow medical advice if it differs from this estimate.

FAQs

How much protein do I need a day?

Many healthy adults need about 0.8 g per kg of body weight; active people often aim for 1.2–1.6 g/kg.

Should I use my current or target weight?

Most guidance uses current weight for people in a healthy range. People with high body fat sometimes use a reference weight; a dietitian can advise.

Is too much protein harmful?

For healthy people moderate excess is generally considered safe, but people with kidney disease should follow medical advice.

Can vegetarians get enough protein?

Yes, by combining dals, dairy, soya, nuts and seeds, and including a protein source at each meal.

Do I need protein supplements?

Not usually. Most people can meet their needs from food.
