## Read an NPS scenario as several separate calculations

### Reconcile the contribution ledger first

The projection begins with the opening amount you enter and adds a regular monthly contribution. At zero return, zero opening balance, 1,000 per month, and one year, the ending corpus is 12,000. The estimated gain is zero. Increase the opening balance to 20,000 while keeping those other assumptions unchanged and the ending corpus becomes 32,000. The new-contribution total remains 12,000; the opening balance is not counted as a new deposit.

This distinction helps when reviewing an existing account. An opening balance already includes its own history of deposits and investment changes. Re-entering past contributions as future monthly payments counts them again. Choose a clear projection start and use the balance from that start as the opening input. The tool is a forward model, not a reconstruction of every transaction since the account was opened.

The contribution-growth field changes the monthly amount once per year. For a 1,000 starting contribution with 10% growth and zero returns, the first year's deposits are 12,000 and the second year's are 13,200. Total new contributions are 25,200. A contribution increase is additional money paid in; it is not part of the investment gain. Keep that separation visible when comparing two projections with different growth settings.

### Treat the return field as an assumption

The annual return describes a smooth scenario within this model. It does not represent a guaranteed crediting rate or predict the sequence of future market gains and losses. An actual account can change because of investment performance and charges as well as your contributions. A single constant rate cannot capture all those details. Use a range of assumptions to understand sensitivity without assigning unwarranted certainty to the largest result.

Monthly deposit timing also affects compounding. Read the timing explained on the page before comparing the estimate with another calculator. A difference may reflect contribution timing or the conversion between annual and monthly rates rather than an incorrect multiplication. Preserve the exact assumption set with the output so a comparison remains meaningful after you change one field.

### Interpret the annuity split as a scenario

An allocation of 40% on the simple 12,000 corpus assigns 4,800 to the illustrative annuity purchase and leaves 7,200 in the non-annuity portion. The sum is still 12,000. These labels divide the modelled corpus; they do not determine which exit option you are entitled to use. Applicable withdrawal rules and product choices must be checked separately with the relevant provider and authority.

At an illustrative annuity rate of 6%, the model calculates 4,800 × 0.06 / 12, or 24 per month. This multiplication is a simplified illustration of income against a purchase amount. It is not an insurer's quote. Actual annuity options can differ by benefits, purchase conditions, and payment arrangements; this tool does not price those contract details or promise an income.

Changing the allocation to zero makes the scenario annuity purchase and illustrative annuity income zero. Changing it to 100% assigns the entire projected corpus to that side of the model. The endpoints are useful arithmetic checks but do not imply that either choice is legally available for a particular account. A slider or valid form input is not an eligibility assessment.

### Separate purchasing power from the account balance

The inflation field discounts the future corpus into today's purchasing-power terms. For a simple check, a nominal corpus of 10,500 one year from now with 5% inflation has a present purchasing-power estimate of 10,000. That is 10,500 divided by 1.05. It does not remove 500 from the account or change the cash actually held at that future date.

A zero-inflation scenario leaves the purchasing-power number equal to the nominal balance. Longer horizons compound the inflation assumption, so the difference can become substantial even when the projected balance grows. The assumed inflation rate and investment return refer to different processes. Neither field should be treated as a prediction simply because the calculator can display a precise decimal result.

### Preserve the projection before comparing providers

Save the opening balance, years, monthly contribution, contribution growth, return, allocation, annuity rate, and inflation together. Check the year-by-year reconciliation before using the annuity illustration in a discussion. The tool does not recommend an asset mix, identify a suitable pension provider, determine tax treatment, or establish a withdrawal right. Its value is the transparent connection between your stated assumptions and the resulting numbers, with those limits kept explicit.
