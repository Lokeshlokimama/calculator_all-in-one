What this calculator does

Enter the appliance’s wattage, how many hours a day it runs, and the number of days (30 by default for a month). The result is the number of units, or kWh, it consumes over that period. Do this for each major appliance to see where your bill is going.

Formula used

Units (kWh) = Watts × Hours per day × Days ÷ 1,000

Worked examples

  • A 75 W ceiling fan running 12 hours a day for 30 days uses 75 × 12 × 30 ÷ 1,000 = 27 units.
  • A 2,000 W geyser used for 1 hour a day uses 2,000 × 1 × 30 ÷ 1,000 = 60 units.

The geyser runs for a twelfth of the time but uses more than twice the energy, which is a typical finding when you list appliances one by one.

Making a household energy list

  1. Write down each appliance and its wattage from the label.
  2. Estimate hours per day honestly; for ACs and fridges, use the time the compressor actually runs, or the BEE star label figure.
  3. Run each through this calculator and add the units.
  4. Compare the total with your last bill. If your list is far lower, something is running more than you think, or there is a fault.

Understanding each input

  • Watts — the appliance’s power rating.
  • Hours per day — decimals allowed; 20 minutes is about 0.33.
  • Days — 30 for a typical month, or your billing cycle. Many Indian discoms bill every two months, so use about 60 to match a bimonthly bill.

Limitations

The result assumes the appliance draws its rated power whenever it is on. Thermostatic appliances use less. Standby power from TVs, set-top boxes and chargers is small per device but adds up; include it as a few watts for 24 hours if you want a complete picture.

Common mistakes

  • Using 30 days when your discom bills every two months.
  • Counting a fridge as running at full power 24 hours a day.
  • Forgetting appliances used seasonally, such as heaters or coolers.

FAQs

How many units does a fan use per month?

A 75 W fan used 12 hours a day uses about 27 units a month. A BLDC fan of around 30 W uses roughly 11.

What is the difference between this and the kWh calculator?

This one multiplies by days for a monthly or billing-cycle total; the kWh calculator works on a single run time.

How do I turn units into a bill amount?

Use the electricity bill calculator, which applies slab rates and fixed charges.

Why does my bill show more units than my list?

Appliances may run longer than estimated, older appliances use more than their label, and wiring faults or leakage add consumption.

Should I count standby power?

For accuracy, yes. A few watts left on all day becomes a few units a month per device.
