What this calculator does

Enter how many units (kWh) you use per day, the average peak sun hours at your location, and the overall system efficiency. The calculator returns the panel capacity needed, in kW and watts, to generate that much energy on an average day.

Formula used

Panel capacity (W) = Daily usage (kWh) × 1,000 ÷ (Peak sun hours × Efficiency)

Peak sun hours are the equivalent number of hours of full-strength sunlight per day. Efficiency covers real-world losses from heat, dust, wiring and the inverter.

Worked example

A household uses 300 units a month, about 10 units a day. With 5 peak sun hours and 80% efficiency:

  • Capacity = 10 × 1,000 ÷ (5 × 0.80) = 2,500 W, or 2.5 kW

In practice that would be rounded to a standard size, such as a 3 kW system made of panels of around 540 W each.

Finding your daily usage

Take the units from your last 12 bills, add them, and divide by 365. Using a year evens out summer AC use and winter months. If your discom bills every two months, divide each bill’s units by about 60 for a daily figure.

Many Indian installers use a rule of thumb that 1 kW of rooftop solar generates about 4 units a day on average. That matches 5 sun hours at 80% efficiency, so the defaults here give similar answers.

Understanding each input

  • Daily usage — average kWh per day you want solar to cover. Enter less if you only want to offset part of your bill.
  • Peak sun hours — usually 4.5 to 6 across most of India. Rainy or hazy regions are lower.
  • Efficiency — 75–85% is typical for a well-installed grid-tied system. It must not exceed 100.

Assumptions and limitations

This gives a yearly-average size. Generation varies by season, roof angle and direction, shading from nearby buildings and trees, and panel cleaning. It does not size batteries for off-grid systems, check whether your roof has space (roughly 6–8 square metres per kW for modern panels), or account for your discom’s net-metering limits. A site survey by an installer is needed before buying. Government subsidy schemes for rooftop solar, such as PM Surya Ghar, have their own capacity rules; check the official portal for current terms.

Common mistakes

  • Sizing from a single summer bill, which oversizes the system.
  • Entering 12 sun hours because the sun is up for 12 hours.
  • Ignoring shade, which can cut output far more than the efficiency figure allows for.

FAQs

How many units does a 1 kW solar system generate in India?

Roughly 3.5–4.5 units a day on average, depending on location, season and installation.

What are peak sun hours?

The number of hours of 1,000 W per square metre sunlight that would deliver the same energy as the day’s actual sunlight.

Does this include batteries?

No. It sizes panels for a grid-tied system. Battery sizing depends on how many hours of backup you need.

How much roof space do I need?

As a rough guide, 6–8 square metres of shade-free roof per kW of modern panels.

Should I size for 100% of my use?

Not necessarily. Net-metering rules, roof space and budget often make a smaller system sensible.
