What this calculator does

Enter the battery’s usable capacity in kWh, your electricity rate per unit, and how much charge you need as a percentage. The calculator estimates the cost of that charge.

Formula used

Cost = Battery capacity (kWh) × Charge needed % ÷ 100 × Rate per kWh

Worked example

An electric car with a 30 kWh battery charged from 20% to 100% (80% needed) at home, where electricity costs 8 per unit:

  • Energy = 30 × 0.80 = 24 kWh
  • Cost = 24 × 8 = 192

Include charging losses

Some energy is lost as heat in the charger and battery. AC home charging typically loses 10–15%, so the meter records more units than the battery stores. With about 12% loss, the example above costs closer to 218. To include losses, divide the result by 0.88, or raise the charge-needed figure.

Home vs public charging

At home you pay your normal tariff, which in India is often 5–10 per unit depending on your state and slab. Charging can push you into a higher slab, so look at your marginal rate. Some states offer a separate EV tariff or time-of-day rates. Public DC fast chargers usually charge a higher rate per kWh plus GST, and sometimes a session or parking fee.

Cost per kilometre

Divide the charging cost by the range you get from that charge. If 24 kWh gives about 200 km of real-world driving, the 218 charge works out to roughly 1.1 per km. Compare that with your petrol or diesel cost using the fuel and trip cost calculator.

Common mistakes

  • Using total battery capacity when the car only lets you use part of it. Use the usable capacity from the specifications.
  • Ignoring charging losses.
  • Comparing a home charging cost with a petrol price without converting both to cost per kilometre.

FAQs

How much does it cost to charge an EV at home in India?

Battery kWh × your per-unit rate, plus about 10–15% for losses. For a 30 kWh car fully charged at 8 per unit, roughly 240–275.

How much does an electric scooter charge cost?

A 3 kWh scooter battery charged fully at 7 per unit costs about 21, plus losses.

Should I charge to 100%?

Many manufacturers suggest 80% for daily use to extend battery life; follow your vehicle’s guidance.

Are public chargers more expensive?

Usually yes, especially DC fast chargers, which add GST and sometimes session fees.

Does this include charging losses?

No. Divide the result by about 0.88 to include typical AC charging losses.
