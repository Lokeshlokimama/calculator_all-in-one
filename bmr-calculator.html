What this calculator does

Basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the energy your body needs to keep running at rest: breathing, circulation, temperature and organ function. Enter your sex, age, weight and height and the calculator estimates BMR in kilocalories per day.

Formula used

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation (1990), widely used in clinical nutrition:

Men: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5

Women: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

Worked examples

  • A 30-year-old man, 70 kg, 175 cm: 700 + 1,093.75 − 150 + 5 = 1,649 kcal/day (1,648.75 rounded).
  • A 30-year-old woman, 60 kg, 160 cm: 600 + 1,000 − 150 − 161 = 1,289 kcal/day.

BMR is not your daily calorie need

You burn more than your BMR every day, because you move, digest food and work. Total daily energy expenditure is often estimated by multiplying BMR by an activity factor: about 1.2 for mostly sitting, rising to about 1.7–1.9 for very active people. For the man above, a lightly active day (about 1.375) would be roughly 2,270 kcal. These multipliers are rough, and individual needs vary.

BMR is not a target to eat to. Eating at or below BMR for long periods is not a healthy plan for most people; a doctor or registered dietitian can help if you are trying to change weight.

How accurate is it?

Mifflin-St Jeor is one of the more accurate equations for adults, but it estimates the average for people of your size. Body composition matters: two people with the same weight and height can differ because muscle uses more energy than fat. Some studies suggest these equations can overestimate BMR in some South Asian populations. Treat the result as a starting point. The equation is designed for adults, not children or pregnant women.

Understanding each input

  • Sex — the equation has separate constants for men and women.
  • Age in years, weight in kilograms, height in centimetres. To convert, 1 inch = 2.54 cm and 1 lb ≈ 0.454 kg.

FAQs

What is a normal BMR?

It depends on size, age and sex. Many adult women fall around 1,200–1,500 kcal/day and many adult men around 1,500–1,900.

Is BMR the same as RMR?

They are close. BMR is measured under stricter conditions; RMR is slightly higher. The Mifflin-St Jeor result is often described as resting energy.

Why does BMR drop with age?

Mostly because muscle mass tends to fall with age, and the equation reflects that with −5 kcal per year.

Should I eat my BMR in calories?

No. BMR is the energy used at rest. Daily needs are higher once activity is included; ask a professional before restricting intake.

Does it work for children?

No. It is for adults.
